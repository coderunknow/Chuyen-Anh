### #26 — 2026-10-13 — DONE
Implemented the distractor-quality improvement selected in entry #25. One hypothesis was tested and **rejected before any code was written**: a suspected systematic bias that placed the correct answer away from option 1 did not exist — over 48 real questions in the unmodified app the answer landed in each slot `[9, 12, 11, 16]`, i.e. `shuffle` is a uniform Fisher–Yates shuffle. Nothing was changed on the basis of that guess, and this entry records the refutation rather than a “fix”.

The real defect was measured instead: in the real application only **38.2%** of the three distractors shared a part of speech with the headword (55/144; exact-string comparison 25.7%), so most options were eliminable at a glance and a word → Vietnamese-gloss question was close to a free pick. Data measurements that shaped the fix: 910 of 913 records have at least three same-part-of-speech peers, only `grammar` (2 records) and `conj` (1 record) do not; 5 glosses are shared by exactly 2 entries each, which the previous filter could surface as two identical options; and the cloze builder never checked its options against the sentence it displayed.

Implementation (`index.html`, question composition only): `posTokens()`/`sharesPos()` match free-text `pos` values by token overlap so `v/n` shares a class with both `v` and `n`; `distractors(vocabulary, current, field, { usable })` builds a same-part-of-speech pool and an other-part-of-speech pool, shuffles each, and takes same-POS first so the fallback only applies where the dataset is too small; candidates whose normalized text repeats the answer or another option are skipped, which removes the duplicate-gloss problem; `wordsIn()` plus the cloze `usable` filter keeps words already printed in the displayed sentence out of the options. No vocabulary record, scheduler rule, storage key, persisted field, import/export field, dependency or workflow changed.

Measured effect with the same probe on the real app, before and after: same-POS distractors 38.2% → **100%** (144/144), with the correct-answer slot distribution staying effectively uniform (`[15, 14, 9, 10]` → `[10, 10, 14, 14]`), and no duplicate option text in either run.

## 2026-10-21 — beta3: “Học cấp tốc” becomes a retrieval method, not a faster animation

### User Intent

The learner-facing goal is to reach the **same predefined mastery criterion in at least 30 % less active study time, targeting ≥ 50 %**, compared with beta2 — with a quality safeguard of no more than a 5 percentage-point absolute drop in delayed first-attempt recall measured at a review scheduled at least one day later. Fewer words, easier questions, skipped reviews, reduced practice or a lowered mastery bar do not count as a speed gain. The design review for this release was explicitly told not to assume that removing waiting, auto-advancing or a faster synthetic benchmark means faster vocabulary acquisition, and not to treat the audit's example redesigns as pre-approved. Turns per word were allowed to rise, under a hard cap, with session size held at N distinct words and at most three turns per word (first pass + consolidation + one correction). The approved mechanics were a hybrid: a per-word adaptive retrieval format, plus one spaced re-retrieval at production level for words that were missed or only recognised; scheduling and first-pass accuracy keep their current inputs, consolidation turns are practice-class, and no metric may be inflated.

### Implementation History

### Decisions & Lessons

- **A faster session is not a faster learner.** Beta2 removed dead time; beta3 adds retrieval work. In the same synthetic workload the sprint now takes **51.1 % longer per session** (20,905 ms / 21 turns vs 13,831 ms / 11 turns) while being **20.8 % faster per turn** (995 ms vs 1,257 ms). Reporting that as “faster learning” would be exactly the confusion the design review forbade, so the benchmark is labelled throughput evidence only, and the 30 %/50 % target and the ≤ 5 pp delayed-recall margin are recorded as unverified without a real learner study and a delayed review.
- **The cap is structural, not a promise.** `consolidationQueued` and `practiceQueued` make a second extra turn impossible, and a simulation over a worst-case queue (every word missed) drains at exactly three turns per word and `size * 3` turns in total. That test would fail if any future path queued a second consolidation or a retry for a consolidation.
- **Clicks were deliberately given back.** The beta2 hand-off now refuses consolidations and revealed self-assessment. Making the delayed check auto-advance would have erased the only moment its feedback is read, and would have inflated the impression of speed while removing evidence.
- **One near-miss in the queue model.** `queueConsolidation` first inserted at `gap + 1` unconditionally, which could place a delayed check *after* the trailing corrective retries and starve it at the end of a session. It now walks back over trailing practice turns. The browser suite caught the visible symptom (a consolidation too close to the end) and the static test pins the ordering.
- **Cross-version comparison is now invalid, and the harness says so.** Comparing beta2's 11-turn sprint with beta3's 21-turn sprint on elapsed time would compare different amounts of work. `--matrix` therefore runs one build only, and the contract rejects a result whose workload does not match its flow.
- **What is still unproven.** No retention, transfer or study-time claim is made; the ladder's ordering (recognition → recall → spelling) is a defensible design, not a measured optimum, and the consolidation gap of three cards is a fixed heuristic. Those two parameters are the first things a learner study should vary.

### Specification of the 5-Part Empirical Learner Study Protocol (Unexecuted; Required Before Any Retention or Learning-Speed Claim)

Software benchmarks measure **UI interaction overhead and queue invariants only** (e.g., Sprint reduces per-turn UI interaction time from `1,274 ms` to `999 ms` at `600/450 ms`, while total session elapsed time rises from `14,015 ms` over `11` turns to `20,984 ms` over `21` turns because `10` delayed recall/spelling consolidations are added). Synthetic timings **cannot** prove that learners reach durable mastery in 30%–50% less active study time or within a `<= 5` percentage-point delayed-recall margin. Establishing or refuting that hypothesis requires executing the following pre-specified learner study outside the static app (no telemetry or backend is added to `index.html`):

1. **Matched Word Sets & Uniform Mastery Criterion:** Randomly assign real target learners (Grade 9 Chuyên Anh applicants) in a counterbalanced within-subjects crossover design across two difficulty- and POS-matched sets of previously unknown words (verified via a baseline pre-test where pre-known items are excluded and replaced from matched strata). Both `Standard` (`mixed`) and `Sprint` (`sprint`) conditions must target the exact same operational mastery criterion (unassisted productive recall + spelling accuracy on delayed testing), with zero reduction in target word count or grading strictness.
2. **Active Study Time Definition:** Define active study time per word set strictly as the sum of active on-task interaction intervals from presentation of the first card (`first-look` or prompt render) through final session summary across all study sessions completed prior to the delayed post-test, recorded locally by supervised study timers or exported local session logs with explicit learner consent.
3. **Idle, Tab-Background, and Break Handling:** Bound idle gaps cleanly and symmetrically across both arms: any inter-action pause exceeding `30 s` without input or any `visibilitychange` hidden interval is logged separately as off-task/break time rather than silently deleted; analyze both **capped active time** (primary endpoint, clamping pauses `> 30 s` identically in both arms) and **unfiltered wall-clock session time** (sensitivity analysis) so neither condition can hide cognitive effort inside uncounted pauses.
4. **Delayed Recall Evaluation (`>= 24 hours` Post-Study, Unassisted First Attempt Only):** Administer an unannounced, unassisted post-test **at least `24 hours`** (and optionally `7 days`) after completion of the final study session, testing every assigned word **once** on **first-attempt productive recall/spelling without hints, multiple-choice options, or self-assessment overrides**. Score only that first attempt per word.
5. **Effect Size & Uncertainty Reporting:** Report both **absolute differences** (minutes of active study time per 10 mastered words; percentage-point difference in `>= 24h` first-attempt recall) and **relative ratios**, accompanied by paired two-sided **95% confidence intervals** (bootstrap or paired t/Wilcoxon intervals clustered by learner and item). Only claim non-inferior retention if the lower 95% confidence bound on `Sprint - Standard` delayed recall exceeds `-5.0 percentage points`, and only claim faster learning if active time per delayed-recalled word drops significantly alongside non-inferior recall.

### Decisions & Lessons

10. **"Premium Academic" redesign.** A new design-system layer appended to the single stylesheet (`/tmp` authoring helper, not committed) defines colour, type (`--font-serif` display face + system UI text), spacing, radius, shadow, hit-size, breakpoint and motion tokens for light and `[data-theme="dark"]`, and covers topbar, navigation, hero, priority panel, chips, segmented control, cards, flashcards, options, timer, pending chips, matching board, summary, dashboard, settings, search and exam reader. Themes are `system | light | dark`, default to `prefers-color-scheme`, and a manual choice is stored in `chuyen-anh.theme.v1`. A 320 px English layout regression (the header's "Backup" label pushing the bar 3 px past the viewport) was found by `browser-exams.cjs` and fixed in the narrow breakpoint.
11. **"Dynamic" motion, bounded.** Immediate right/wrong feedback, animated progress, hover/press states and card/match/summary transitions run on CSS only. Confetti is drawn on one canvas inside `#confetti-layer` and only for real milestones (a new badge, a perfect session of ≥ 5 graded words); a launch token cancels any previous frame loop, and `prefers-reduced-motion` disables the animation entirely.
12. **Localization of every new string.** The English dictionary now holds 554 exact entries and 92 anchored patterns (up from the beta4 set), covering the priority panel, configuration, settings, dashboard, timer, matching board, pending list, ambiguity panel, XP/badges and the new toasts. The new copy was restructured so each translatable phrase is a single text node (counts moved out of `<strong>`), because the localizer translates text nodes and refuses to guess inside sentences. `browser-learning.cjs` now walks the rendered DOM in English on the study home, Settings, the dashboard and a live session and asserts that no Vietnamese text survives outside the protected content classes and the product name.
13. **Performance fix found by a user-visible stall.** Opening the priority panel (on the home screen and in Settings) blocked the main thread for ~3.6 s: the per-word availability check built and shuffled the full distractor list for every word on every render, which the CDP sampling profiler attributed to `distractors` (≈2.1 s) and `normalize` (≈1.0 s). The check now short-circuits on the first distinct candidate and indexes part-of-speech tokens once in a `Set`: the same render costs ≈4 ms.

### Decisions & Lessons

- **The focus is a selection rule, not a scheduler rule.** Nothing in the priority path writes to a progress record; the focus quota only decides *which* eligible words are drawn. Tests assert both the allocation change and the byte-identical records, because "the focus changes selection" and "the focus changes scheduling" are easy to conflate.
- **Every mode must be able to say "no".** Availability is computed from real data and reported as counts before the session starts; a mode with no usable words is disabled with its count instead of silently falling back to another type. Cloze stays off by default precisely because the dataset supports it for one word.
- **Ambiguity is a first-class grading outcome.** Two entries sharing a gloss means a typed answer can be evidence without being either right or wrong; the app asks, it does not guess.
- **Additive backup fields beat a version bump.** Keeping `version: 1` and treating an absent block as "no opinion" means a beta4 file still imports, and restoring one cannot silently delete a new list. The trade-off is documented in README: the new blocks are optional and older builds ignore them.
- **Localization is a rendering constraint, not a lookup table.** Strings split across inline elements cannot be translated safely; the new markup keeps one phrase per text node, which is why some bold counts became plain text.
- **Tests found four real defects** (disabled-group counts, back-to-back boards, unreachable English ambiguity branch, empty-session XP/history) and one performance regression (distractor construction) before any of them reached a learner.

## 2026-10-23 — v1.0.0-beta6: the priority start that did nothing, and the audit behind it

### User Intent

A learner reported that pressing **“Bắt đầu phiên ưu tiên”** showed the press animation but nothing else happened. The request was to fix that, audit the rest of the app for serious defects, and ship the result (merge + Pages update).

### Implementation History

1. **Reproduced, then located, the reported symptom.** The button works from the study home (it was covered by the beta5 browser scenario), so the report had to come from another entry point. The learner stayed on Settings while a hidden session (and, with the timer on, a hidden countdown) ran in the background. The same omission existed in `startSession()`, which is how the dashboard’s “Từ yếu” card starts a session.
2. **The XP baseline belonged to only one of the three session starters.** `sessionXpStart` was assigned inside `startPrioritySession()` only, so any classic or sprint session computed “XP earned this session” against whatever value was left from an earlier priority session — or `0`. Measured on the shipped beta5 build: a 10-word quick session that earned 61 XP displayed **“XP nhận trong phiên +10861”** with a 10,800-XP profile, because the baseline was zero. The summary now equals `xpTotal − xpBefore` for every starter.
3. **Session-scoped state leaked between sessions.** `startSession()` reset neither `skippedIds` nor `matchState`, so a new session opened with the previous session’s skipped-question chips (and counted them as unanswered). A shared reset now runs in all three starters.

Hãy bật thêm nhóm từ hoặc đổi trọng tâm.”), translated in English.

7. **“Học tiếp” left the shell stale.** The summary’s continue button re-rendered only the content pane, so the due count, streak and XP in the shell kept the finished session’s numbers until the next navigation. It now re-renders the shell.

### Decisions & Lessons

- **A session is only real where the learner can see it.** Every entry point now owns three things explicitly: the view it lands on, the XP baseline its summary measures against, and a clean session state. Those three are pinned by structural guards on the shipped module and by browser scenarios that start sessions from Settings, the dashboard and the home panel.
- **A blocked primary action must explain itself.** Semantically disabling the start button is honest but useless after a click; the button therefore stays in the tab order, points at the explanation, and answers when activated. This is the second half of the reported symptom, and the reason the fix is not just `view = 'learn'`.
- **The reported bug was narrower than the class of bug.** The user saw one dead button; the same omission affected a second starter and three different properties. Auditing by *entry point* rather than by *symptom* is what surfaced the XP and state-leak defects.
- **In-place refresh beats a full re-render when the user is typing.** A shell re-render is simple and correct for lists, but it silently discards unsubmitted input; the two toggles that can be pressed mid-answer now update just themselves.

### Decisions & Lessons

- **A count is only as good as its rejection rule.** The 3,396-row build satisfied the numeric target and was wrong: it double-counted 218 derived forms the app already teaches. Making the rule executable (and reporting every skip) is what turned an inflated number into a defensible 3,215.
- **Two data sets need two IDs and two stores, or they will be confused forever.** `P###` versus numeric IDs, `chuyen-anh.phrases.v1` versus `chuyen-anh.progress.v1`, and a separate practice queue make the separation structural rather than a naming convention; the tests assert the word store is byte-identical after a phrase session.
- **Provenance belongs in a field, not in prose.** `NOTES` keeps the human explanation; `SOURCES` is machine-checkable, registry-validated and honest about the difference between "this phrase occurs in a transcribed paper at this line" and "this dictionary entry exists at this URL".
- **Optional blocks are how a v1 format grows.** The phrase block is written only when it has content and is read only when present, so old files, new files and older apps all continue to work without a version bump.
- **A 54 % larger HTML file is a real cost.** The benchmark shows no interaction regression, but the single file grew from 5.33 MB to 8.23 MB; the phrase bank and the extra vocabulary are now most of that payload, and a future split would be a deployment decision, not a data decision.
- **Release boundaries must be enforceable, not remembered.** `DEPLOY_HOLD` = `v1.0.0-beta7` with no beta7 release means the gate itself keeps Pages on the last published build; restoring the beta4 prerelease cannot change that outcome.

## 2026-10-24 — one headword per root: the vocabulary counts words, not forms

**Trigger.** The maintainer's instruction: *"Những từ cùng gốc từ thì chỉ tính riêng 1 từ và có ghi chú thêm thôi chứ không có tính là từ riêng biệt được."* Beta7 had just shipped 3,215 headwords, and a share of them were forms of a word already taught — `abundance` next to `abundant`, `consistently` next to `consistent`. Counting both inflates the number and asks the learner to study the same root twice.

**Decision.** A word root is one vocabulary item. Its other forms are a note on that item (`WORD_FAMILY` → `Cùng gốc: <form> (<pos>: <meaning>)`) and are **not** headwords, **not** schedulable cards, **not** counted. The merged rows are **kept** in the table and in `data/vocabulary.json` as audit records — same ID, same content, plus `family:merged` and `family-head:<id>` — because deleting rows would destroy the audit trail and leave IDs that could later be reused by a different word. The app filters them out at load (2,684 of 3,215 rows) and folds any progress stored against them onto the headword.

**Why the map is hand-reviewed data and not code.** Every algorithmic attempt was worse than the manual map: suffix-only stems merged `Apparent/appear/apply/appliance/appeal`; union-find over aggressive roots produced 339 noisy components; a 5-letter-prefix scan produced 350 groups, most of them false (`deliver/live`, `note/notify`, `readily/ready`). So `docs/vocabulary-families.json` holds 380 reviewed families (509 forms) with a `kind` per family (`derived`, `same root`, `inflection`), and `scripts/vocabulary_families.py` only applies and validates it. Applying it inside `expand_vocabulary.py` means the Markdown table, the JSON and the embedded HTML cannot drift apart.

**False friends were rejected before shipping.** The first draft of the map also grouped 22 forms that are separate dictionary entries, or that only look alike: `virtue ← Virtually` (Virtually belongs to *virtual*), `Principle ← principal`, `person ← people`, `quality ← Qualification`, `define ← definitely`, `technology ← technically`, `organise ← organic`, `prior`, `regular`, `Competent`, `population`, `Variation`, `literal/literary/literature`, `conceive`, `Distinguish`, `expedition`, `attendance/attendant`, `integrity`. Each was removed and the row became a headword again, which is why the shipped map is 380 families / 509 forms and not 392 / 531; `docs/vocabulary-families.json` records them under `reviewed.rejectedMerges`. `note ← notification, notify` also had to be re-headed as `notify ← notification`, because `notify` is a row of its own and a family cannot swallow the headword of another. This is the same failure mode as the rejected heuristics — a plausible-looking stem — caught by reading the note the learner would actually see (`Cùng gốc: Virtually` under *virtue* is obviously wrong on the screen, and obviously fine in a diff).

**Dangling heads were re-pointed, not dropped.** The curated map named `produce`, `govern`, `infect` and `substantial` as heads, but none of them has a row in the data. Rather than silently losing those families, they now hang off an existing headword that is genuinely the root: `government ← governance`, `infection ← infectious`, `substance ← substantial, substantially`, `temperature ← temperate`.

**Bookkeeping is not evidence.** The dev4/dev5 ledgers were frozen before this rule existed and audit the 913 legacy rows field by field. Adding a tag and a generated note to those rows is a bookkeeping change, not a dictionary finding, so `audit_view()` strips `family:*` tags and the `Cùng gốc:` note before comparison. Rewriting a historical evidence ledger to match a new form of bookkeeping would have been the wrong fix; normalizing the comparison is the honest one.

**Progress survives the merge.** `strip_family_bookkeeping()` runs before the map is applied, so a family edited out of the map leaves no stale tag or note behind; without it the second run would inherit its own output. `foldFamilyProgress` moves a stored record from a merged form to its headword: counts add, streak/interval keep the furthest value, `lastReviewed` keeps the most recent date, and `nextReview` keeps the **earlier** date, so a merge can never delay a review. `state` is re-derived from the combined counts. Merged-row IDs never disappear from the dataset, so no ID can be reused by a future word — the property that makes the folding safe. `familyHeadMap` is built once per load and reused for favorites and flags.

**The count is now honest, and the old target is not met.** Beta7 reported 3,215 headwords against a ≥3,000 target. Those 3,215 were *rows*, 509 of which duplicate a root; the true headword count is **2,706**. `README.md` says the 3,000-root target is **not** reached and what reaching it would require (≥294 new roots, with derived forms not counting). Publishing the smaller, correct number is the point of the exercise; a count that only survives because forms are double-counted is not a vocabulary measure.

- **A candidate list is not a dictionary.** The corpus tells us a form occurs in a real exam; it says nothing about what the form means or which CEFR level it belongs to. - **The honest number is the one after the rule is applied.** 5,852 rows is not the vocabulary size; 5,205 roots is. The docs, the app label and the tests all say 5,205.

## 2026-10-25 (review round 4) — making the review configuration part of the repository

### The decision

The two previous review rounds depended on account-level CodeRabbit defaults rather than on anything this repository declared, and the most direct evidence of that was a commit status reading "Review skipped: draft pull request" on a head that had asked for review. The fix is a committed `.coderabbit.yaml`: every push to every open pull request, drafts included, any target branch, no commit-count pause and no title/author/label escape hatches.

### Why the file is small

- **Only two exclusions, both machine-generated.** `docs/vocabulary-audit-dev5.json` and `docs/vocabulary-300-counts.json` are rewritten wholesale by `scripts/generate_vocab_audit_dev5.py` and `scripts/audit_learned_vocab.py`, both of which fail a `--check` run when stale. Reviewing their line diff buries the change that caused it. Their generators, their inputs and the prose report stay in scope.
- **Nothing that carries product meaning was excluded.** `index.html` is the shipped application and the source of truth for its embedded data; `data/vocabulary.json` and `data/phrases.json` are what the app actually serves; the exam transcriptions are the audit's subject. Excluding any of them to make reviews smaller would have traded correctness for convenience.
- **No allowlist.** Only exclusions are configured, so files added later are reviewed by default rather than silently skipped — the failure mode of a fragile include-only filter.

### Trade-offs stated rather than discovered later

- **Every automatic incremental review consumes the same allowance as a manual one.** With the pause disabled and drafts included, an active branch can spend the hourly allowance quickly; when it runs out CodeRabbit reports a rate-limited review instead of reviewing. That is the requested behaviour, written into the file so a future maintainer sees it before changing it.
- **Filtering files does not save allowance.** The limit is per review, not per file; the exclusions shorten a review, they do not buy another one.
- **No scheduled reviews.** The schema has no such key — scheduling is an account setting — so the file cannot and does not claim to enable it.

### Verification of the configuration itself

A configuration file that is merely plausible is worse than none, so the committed file was asserted rather than eyeballed: parsed with duplicate-key detection, every mandatory `auto_review` value compared against the requirement, `abort_on_close` checked, and the two globs run through `minimatch` against a list of files that must be excluded and a list that must stay in scope. The lists, not just the file, are the evidence.

### One documentation defect found by the review of the previous head

The D-18 passage quoted the removed assertion inside a single-backtick span while the quoted text contained two more backticks, so the span closed early and rendered as prose (MD038). Fixed by moving the assertion into a fenced block. It is a good reminder that the audit report is itself a rendering surface: a claim can be true and still be unreadable, and the review caught a defect the report's own tests could not.

### Round 4 addendum — a configuration claim is a claim

The first review that ran under the committed `.coderabbit.yaml` did something a configuration file cannot do for itself: it checked whether the file's prose matched its effect. The comment said the seed TSVs stayed in scope; CodeRabbit excludes `**/*.tsv` by default, and neither configured exclusion touched that default, so the sentence was false for exactly the files it named.

Two lessons, both familiar from earlier rounds in a different disguise:

## 2026-10-28 (third follow-up) — the red CI step, and the slowest 24 ms in the app

### User Intent

The previous CI run of the new `browser` job failed at `tests/browser-learning.cjs:761` while the same suite passed locally. Nothing
was committed for it because the cause was unknown. Two questions had to be answered before touching anything: is the app wrong, or
is the test wrong — and why is the `checks` job (which runs the static suite) 12 minutes long.

### Implementation History

1. **Read the failure from the only accessible source.** GitHub's job logs sit behind a blob URL this sandbox cannot open, so the
   evidence was the annotation dump added to the workflow: `AssertionError: a revealed spelling card cannot be typed into`, at
   `tests/browser-learning.cjs:761`, after the preceding `isEnabled()` assertion had passed.
2. **Ruled the application out by reading the render path.** `locked = answered || revealed` writes `disabled` into both the input
   and its confirm button on the reveal re-render, and the document handler refuses Space when the event target is an `INPUT`. So
   the only way to get that assertion is: the reveal never happened, because Space was typed into the field.
3. **Measured the ordering instead of guessing.** A scratch probe stamped every `focusin` with `performance.now()`, then replayed
   the suite's sequence. The card's `setTimeout(…, 0)` focus lands **4–16 ms after render**, and the test's `blur()` follows it —
   so locally focus is already in the input and the blur succeeds. Delaying that task made the failure reproducible: when the
   focus landed **2 ms after the blur**, `document.activeElement` was `#spelling-input` at press time, `.reveal` was absent and the
   input was still enabled — the CI signature exactly. With the timer landing anywhere else, the old sequence passed.
4. **Fixed the test, not the app.** All five Space-shortcut steps now call `pressSpaceShortcut()`, which waits for the card's focus
   to land (bounded), blurs, retries if the card takes focus back, asserts that the document owns the key, and only then presses.
   The application was not changed: a human cannot type inside that window, and moving focus into the input is the intended
   behaviour.
5. **Then measured the static suite, which is why `checks` takes 12 minutes.** `node --test tests/static-app.test.js` never
   finished within a 15-minute window on this machine; one test — "every multiple-choice question offers plausible, distinct
   options" — took **443 s** on its own.
6. **Profiled before optimising.** `generateQuestion` for 17.556 (record, mode) calls cost **425 s**, i.e. **24 ms median per card**
   (200 cards, mean 25,1 ms, `p90` 30 ms). Inside it, `distractors()` re-keys every one of the 5.852 rows on every question:
   `normalize` + `normalizeTyped` per candidate (the NFC pass in `normalizeTyped` dominates), plus a part-of-speech split per
   candidate. The test's own peer-count loop (28 s) and `Array.find` per option (2,4 s) were secondary.
7. **Memoised the pure parts.** The deck is immutable — nothing in `index.html` assigns to `word`, `meaning` or `pos` — so the two
   option keys per row and field and the part-of-speech tokens are cached in `WeakMap`s (`optionKeys`, `posTokens`). The
   candidate rules are untouched: same keys, same exclusions, same shuffle.
8. **Proved equivalence rather than assuming it.** A scratch harness loaded the previous `index.html` from `git show HEAD:index.html`
   and the new one in the same process, replayed one seeded `Math.random` for both, and compared option ids, order and correctness:
   **0 mismatches over 17.556 pairs**. The independent static assertion (same-POS distractor count computed from the data) still
   passes for every row.
9. **Made the test-side work cheap too,** with the same rule: the peer-text union is memoised per part-of-speech token set and the
   two formulations were compared on all 17.556 pairs before the change (0 differences); option lookup by id index replaces
   `Array.find`.
10. **Verification on the final tree:** pipeline `--check` × 12 clean, `python -m unittest discover` **130 OK**, `node --test
    tests/static-app.test.js` **114/114 in 1m54s** (was ~7,5 min), both browser suites PASS sequentially on Chromium 133, and the
    suites were re-run *after* the application change.

### Decisions & Lessons

- **"It failed in CI" is a hypothesis until the ordering is measured.** The first instinct — the app's focus timer is stealing
  focus — was right, but only the timestamped probe showed *where* in the sequence it lands and why the same line passes locally.
  Without that, the tempting "fix" would have been to weaken the assertion.
- **A race in a test is still a defect in the test.** The helper waits for the state it depends on and asserts the precondition it
  needs ("the document, not an input, owns the key"), so the next failure names its own cause instead of presenting as an app bug.
- **Profile the function, not the file.** The suite looked like "a slow test file"; the profile showed 96 % of the time inside one
  application function called 17.556 times. Optimising the test's own loops first would have shaved 30 s of 443 s.
- **A cache is only safe if the data is immutable.** The `WeakMap` keys rely on nothing assigning to `word`/`meaning`/`pos`; that
  was checked in the source before the change, not assumed.
- **A faster implementation needs an equality proof, not a green suite.** The seeded-random replay compares the actual option sets
  the learner would see, which a passing test suite cannot show.
- **Still open:** the first card of a session pays the cache fill (~54 ms, unchanged); the visible card transition was not re-timed
  in a browser; the vestigial `curationCategories` key in `vocabulary_evidence_review.build_report()` is still there; 923 vs 930

## 2026-10-28 (fourth follow-up) — an opt-in diagnostics log the learner sends themselves

### User Intent

The user asked for "a telemetry feature too, log and be able to send the log to me by the users", with users able to turn it on and off
manually and legally, and to be asked again before merge/tag/release. So the feature must be (a) genuinely optional, (b) legal in the
sense of consent-based and documented, (c) able to reach the maintainer, and (d) unable to quietly collect learner content.

### Implementation History

1. **Decided the transport before writing code.** The app is one HTML file with no backend, no network and a hard rule against adding
   a dependency. So there is **no endpoint and no automatic send**: the app writes a bounded local log, and the learner downloads a
   fixed-shape JSON report and forwards it (the panel links to the project's issues). This is the only design that satisfies "send it
   to me" without inventing a server, a third party or a silent upload — and a test asserts the app script contains no `fetch`,
   `XMLHttpRequest` or `sendBeacon` at all.
2. **Consent is the gate, not a setting among others.** `chuyen-anh.telemetry-consent.v1` stores `{contract, enabled, decidedAt}`;
   `sanitizeTelemetryConsent` treats `enabled: true` without a valid `decidedAt` as *not* consent, and nothing is read from or written
   to the log store before it. Turning the switch off is a withdrawal, so the log key is **removed** (not emptied).
3. **The event shape is a whitelist.** `kind` must be one of eight fixed values, `name` must match a short token pattern, counters are
   rounded finite numbers capped at six with a 1e9 bound, and `note` is cut at the **first quote character** after stripping control
   characters and e-mail addresses. The first version tried pair-matching quotes; a nested `"từ \"bí mật\""` left learner text behind,
   so the rule became "keep only what precedes the first quote", which is enough for an error kind and carries no content.
4. **Hooks are few and content-free:** boot, session end (turns/correct/wrong/unanswered only), opening Tra cứu, backup export/import
   outcome, consent changes and uncaught errors. Nothing per card, nothing per word, nothing per query.
5. **The report states its own limits.** `buildTelemetryReport` emits product, version, consent decision, a bucketed environment
   (language, theme, mobile/tablet/desktop, reduced motion) and a `notCollected` list naming answers, queries, backup contents, user
   agent, device ids, timezone, IP and exact viewport. Environment fields are whitelisted, so a caller cannot leak a user agent in.
6. **Legal documentation.** New `PRIVACY.md` (Vietnamese + English summary): what is recorded, in a table; what is never recorded; the
   lawful basis (explicit opt-in consent); the learner's rights (view, download, delete, withdraw) and how each is exercised in the UI.
   The panel carries a short version, and its full policy paragraph is a single text node so the English interface translates it.
7. **Tests.** Static: *the diagnostics log is opt-in, local, bounded and free of learner content* — 30 assertions covering consent
   defaults, the undated-consent reject, scrubbing (including the nested-quote case), counter bounds, the 200-event cap, immutability,
   `removeItem` erasure, report shape, the `notCollected` list, and the absence of any transmission path. Browser: default-off with no
   key on a device that never consented, consent record with timestamp, real events from a search visit with **no query text inside**,
   a downloaded report (`chuyen-anh-diagnostics-YYYY-MM-DD.json`) containing no learner text and no user agent, erasure on withdrawal,
   the English panel, and **zero external network requests** while the whole feature is used.
8. **Verification:** static **115/115**, Python **130 OK**, both browser suites PASS sequentially (the learning suite now takes ~6m30s).
   Preview served from the working tree on port 8000 for the user to try the switch.

### Decisions & Lessons

- **"Telemetry" without a server is a file, not a beacon.** Sending the log required inventing infrastructure the project forbids, so the
  honest feature is: record locally, let the learner send it. That is also the only version where "on/off manually" is meaningful.
- **Consent must be a precondition, not a preference.** A boolean sitting next to theme and language would have been collected by whatever
  wrote the log first; a separate versioned record with a decision time makes "off" the default state of the store itself.
- **Whitelists beat scrubbers.** The event shape accepts only known keys and numeric counters, so a new call site cannot accidentally
  start shipping a word or a query; the note scrubber exists only for error messages and is deliberately destructive.
- **A quote-matching scrubber is not a scrubber.** The nested-quote failure was found by writing the adversarial case into the test first;
  cutting at the first quote is lossy for prose and safe for content.
- **Still open:** no endpoint, so delivery is manual by design; the log is per browser profile, not per user; nothing was merged, tagged,
  released or deployed, and the merge/tag/release decision is waiting on the user.
