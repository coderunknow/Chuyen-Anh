### #26 — 2026-10-13 — DONE
Implemented the distractor-quality improvement selected in entry #25. One hypothesis was tested and **rejected before any code was written**: a suspected systematic bias that placed the correct answer away from option 1 did not exist — over 48 real questions in the unmodified app the answer landed in each slot `[9, 12, 11, 16]`, i.e. `shuffle` is a uniform Fisher–Yates shuffle. Nothing was changed on the basis of that guess, and this entry records the refutation rather than a “fix”.

The real defect was measured instead: in the real application only **38.2%** of the three distractors shared a part of speech with the headword (55/144; exact-string comparison 25.7%), so most options were eliminable at a glance and a word → Vietnamese-gloss question was close to a free pick. Data measurements that shaped the fix: 910 of 913 records have at least three same-part-of-speech peers, only `grammar` (2 records) and `conj` (1 record) do not; 5 glosses are shared by exactly 2 entries each, which the previous filter could surface as two identical options; and the cloze builder never checked its options against the sentence it displayed.

Implementation (`index.html`, question composition only): `posTokens()`/`sharesPos()` match free-text `pos` values by token overlap so `v/n` shares a class with both `v` and `n`; `distractors(vocabulary, current, field, { usable })` builds a same-part-of-speech pool and an other-part-of-speech pool, shuffles each, and takes same-POS first so the fallback only applies where the dataset is too small; candidates whose normalized text repeats the answer or another option are skipped, which removes the duplicate-gloss problem; `wordsIn()` plus the cloze `usable` filter keeps words already printed in the displayed sentence out of the options. No vocabulary record, scheduler rule, storage key, persisted field, import/export field, dependency or workflow changed.

Measured effect with the same probe on the real app, before and after: same-POS distractors 38.2% → **100%** (144/144), with the correct-answer slot distribution staying effectively uniform (`[15, 14, 9, 10]` → `[10, 10, 14, 14]`), and no duplicate option text in either run.

## 2026-10-21 — beta2 hand-off: the information moves with the learner, the wait does not

**Type: DECISION + application change + measurement correction.** The beta2 sprint work (carried over from PR #36, which this replaces) removes the 1,600 ms dwell that beta1 spent on every correct first-pass answer. What the dwell was for — letting the learner see what the previous card graded — is now carried onto the next card by a `.handoff-strip` live region (`role="status"`, `aria-live="polite"`) instead of holding the card still. The timer and its whole API are deleted rather than zeroed, the rule is the exported `shouldHandOffImmediately({ mode, correct, practice, revealed })`, and nothing about the learning work changed: wrong answers still stop for acknowledgement, the corrective retry still waits, revealed-answer self-assessment does not hand off, and first-pass accuracy, scheduling and mastery are computed as before.

**Stale-answer adjacency.** Reproduced before fixing: a correct answer left the strip on the following card, and pressing `Bỏ qua` rotated the queue to an unrelated word while the strip kept claiming the earlier word's verdict. A rotation invalidates the adjacency the strip asserts, so `skipCard()` now clears it; the regression test fails without that line. Keeping the strip after navigation inside the session is still correct and still asserted, because there the card is unchanged.

**Measurement correction, stated plainly.** The first benchmark harness measured the dwell as 1,322–1,328 ms and the beta2 change as 7–10 ms, and an earlier log entry read the closeness of the beta1 figure to its 1.6 s constant as corroboration that the harness observed the timer. That was wrong reasoning about a wrong number: the harness stamped the answer *before* Playwright clicked and the card *after* the click promise resolved, so it charged part of the dwell to click overhead. Timing now happens inside the page — a capture-phase listener for the answer, a `MutationObserver` for the actual card change — and beta1 measures 1,611–1,612 ms, consistent with an independent in-page probe of 1,622–1,636 ms. The correction moves the beta1 figure, not the conclusion: the dwell was real, it dominated the sprint, and removing it is what made the sprint faster than the standard flow.

**Re-measured, twelve validated scenarios, three rounds each (Chromium 133):** beta2 sprint is 9,766 / 17,466 / 9,486 ms against 13,806 / 21,646 / 11,790 ms for its own standard flow (−29.3 %, −19.3 %, −19.5 %) and against 23,910 / 31,920 / 23,646 ms for beta1's sprint (−59.2 %, −45.3 %, −59.9 %). Hand-off latency 9 × 9–10 ms versus 9 × 1,611–1,612 ms; the standard flow has no hand-off and needs eleven manual continues, sprint needs two (the miss and the retry). Most of sprint's advantage over the standard flow is the removed per-card button press — a real property of the design, not a measurement artifact — and the comparison remains one of interaction throughput on a fixed synthetic workload. No retention or learning-quality equivalence is claimed; that would need real learners.

**Harness as a gate, not a printer.** The workload invariants now live in dependency-free `tests/benchmark-contract.cjs` and are asserted for every round and every scenario, with a non-zero exit on violation, so a run that graded fewer words or lost the retry cannot be quietly compared. Writing them surfaced two real defects in the harness itself: it accepted its own incomplete runs, and after the final continue click the loop could re-read the same card and grade it twice (observed once as `practiceTurns was 2`).

**Accessibility and keyboard scope.** The strip is a polite live region so the carried answer is announced without stealing focus; focus still lands on the content region after a re-render and the hand-off path needs no click at all. This was verified by assertions on the strip's text, `role` and `aria-live` and by the existing keyboard tests, **not** with a screen reader — that gap is unchanged and still open.
